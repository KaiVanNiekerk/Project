## 1. Project Direction: K2VAE for Probabilistic Forecasting

My project is now centred on K2VAE as a structured probabilistic forecasting model for time-series data. The core question is not simply “can we predict the future?”, but “can we predict the future in a way that is both structured and uncertainty-aware?”

That is why this direction matters. Time-series forecasting is difficult when the underlying dynamics are nonlinear, noisy, and unstable over long horizons. Many standard models can predict one step ahead reasonably well, but they become less reliable when the forecast horizon grows. That is where K2VAE becomes interesting.

The research question I am working with is:

- Can a model that combines Koopman-inspired linearisation, Kalman-style refinement, and variational inference produce better long-horizon probabilistic forecasts than simpler baselines?

This is a strong thesis direction because it sits at the intersection of:

- dynamical systems,
- probabilistic modelling,
- representation learning,
- and sequence forecasting.

---

## 2. Problem Statement

Time-series forecasting becomes especially difficult when the data are generated by nonlinear dynamics. In such settings, the model must not only predict future values but also capture the uncertainty of those future values. If the model is too deterministic, it may give overconfident predictions. If it is too unconstrained, it may fail to learn meaningful temporal structure.

The main difficulties are:

- nonlinear dynamics make the transition process hard to model directly;
- long horizons amplify small errors, causing forecast drift;
- uncertainty needs to be modelled explicitly rather than hidden inside a point estimate;
- and many generative models are computationally expensive for long forecasting tasks.

K2VAE is relevant because it tries to address these issues directly. Instead of treating forecasting as a purely black-box sequence problem, it introduces a dynamical structure that aims to make the temporal evolution easier to model.

---

## 3. Why K2VAE Is Interesting

The main idea of K2VAE is simple but powerful: make nonlinear dynamics easier to forecast by putting them into a representation where they look more linear, then refine that representation using a probabilistic state-space mechanism.

In other words, K2VAE tries to do three things at once:

1. learn a representation in which the dynamics are easier to describe;
2. model the temporal evolution using a structured linear dynamical interpretation;
3. capture predictive uncertainty instead of returning only one deterministic trajectory.

That is what makes it more than just another sequence model. It is an attempt to combine classical dynamical systems reasoning with modern deep probabilistic modelling.

The paper’s contribution is not merely that it predicts well. It is that it does so by using a more structured latent dynamics story than many deep forecasting methods.

## 4. Core Concepts

This section is the conceptual core of the project. Every concept below is included because it is relevant to understanding K2VAE, not because it is a generic machine-learning topic.

### 4.1 Time-Series Forecasting

In forecasting, the goal is to model the conditional distribution of future values given the observed history. For a sequence $X = [x_1, \dots, x_T]$, the model learns a distribution over a future horizon $Y = [x_{T+1}, \dots, x_L]$.

The challenge is that future values are uncertain, so the model should ideally produce a distribution rather than only a single point estimate.

### 4.2 Latent State and State-Space Models

A state-space model assumes that the system has an unobserved internal state $z_t$ that evolves over time. The observed data $x_t$ are generated from this latent state.

A standard structure is:

$$
z_t \sim p(z_t \mid z_{t-1})
$$

and

$$
x_t \sim p(x_t \mid z_t)
$$

This is the right conceptual starting point for K2VAE because the model is fundamentally about latent temporal dynamics. The hidden state is not just a convenience; it is the mechanism through which the model represents the evolution of the system.

### 4.3 The Markov Assumption

A major simplification in state-space modelling is the Markov assumption. It says that the next latent state depends only on the current latent state, not on the entire history of the sequence:

$$
p(z_t \mid z_{1:t-1}) = p(z_t \mid z_{t-1})
$$

This is important because it gives the model a tractable temporal structure. K2VAE uses this idea in spirit: it tries to model evolution through a state transition process rather than through an unconstrained sequence model.

### 4.4 Koopman Theory and Linearisation

Koopman theory is central to K2VAE. The basic idea is that a nonlinear dynamical system can sometimes be viewed through a transformed representation where its evolution becomes approximately linear.

For a nonlinear system

$$
x_{k+1} = f(x_k)
$$

Koopman theory introduces a measurement function $\psi$ such that

$$
\psi(x_{k+1}) = K\psi(x_k)
$$

where $K$ is the Koopman operator. The important intuition is that although the original dynamics may be nonlinear in the original state space, they may be approximately linear in the transformed measurement space.

This is exactly the motivation behind K2VAE: if I can find a representation where the dynamics are easier to model linearly, then forecasting should become more stable and less error-prone.

### 4.5 Measurement Function and the Idea of a Better Coordinate System

The measurement function $\psi$ is one of the most important conceptual pieces in K2VAE. It is the learned map that transforms the input sequence into a space where the temporal dynamics are easier to represent.

The model does not assume that the original observation space is already the best space for forecasting. Instead, it learns a new representation where the dynamics may look simpler.

This is important because forecasting is often easier in a good representation than in the raw observation space.

### 4.6 Kalman Filtering and Kalman-style Refinement

The Kalman filter is a classical method for state estimation in linear Gaussian state-space models. It alternates between a prediction step and an update step. A common form is:

$$
z_k = A z_{k-1} + B u_k + w_k
$$

$$
y_k = H z_k + v_k
$$

where $w_k$ and $v_k$ are process and observation noise. The filter updates the current belief about the latent state using the new observation.

K2VAE does not use the classical Kalman filter exactly as written. Instead, it uses a learned Kalman-style refinement mechanism, which is why the paper calls it a KalmanNet. The model uses the structure of Kalman filtering to refine an imperfect linear system and to model uncertainty over the latent dynamics.

This is crucial because the Koopman-based linearisation is only approximate. The Kalman-style step is what helps the model correct for that approximation.

### 4.7 Variational Inference and the ELBO

K2VAE is also a variational model. That means it does not only learn a deterministic mapping from input to forecast. Instead, it learns a distribution over latent variables.

The core variational objective is the ELBO:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{ELBO} = \mathbb{E}_{q(z\mid x)}[\log p(y\mid z,x)] - \mathrm{KL}(q(z\mid x) \| p(z))
$$

The first term rewards predictive accuracy. The second term regularises the latent distribution so that it does not drift too far from the prior.

This is useful for forecasting because it gives the model a principled way to represent uncertainty rather than simply returning a point prediction.

### 4.8 Reparameterisation Trick

Because the latent variable is sampled from a distribution, the model needs a differentiable way to backpropagate through that sampling step. This is why the reparameterisation trick is used.

Instead of sampling directly from a Gaussian, the model samples from a standard normal and then transforms it:

$$
z = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon,
\quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)
$$

This is relevant because K2VAE is trained end-to-end and needs to learn both the latent distribution and the forecasting distribution jointly.

### 4.9 Patching and Tokenisation

The paper uses patching as an input representation. Instead of treating each time step as an isolated token, the model groups nearby time points into patches. These patches form the basic units from which the temporal dynamics are reasoned about.

This is important because it gives the model a slightly more structured notion of local temporal context. It also helps the model treat time series as a sequence of meaningful blocks rather than as a long chain of individual points.

### 4.10 Probabilistic Forecasting Metrics

Because the model is probabilistic, evaluation should not focus only on mean error. The important quantities are:

- CRPS: measures the quality of the full predictive distribution;
- NMAE: measures point-forecast error in a normalised form;
- calibration: whether the predicted uncertainty is appropriate;
- stability over long horizons: whether the forecast remains reliable as the horizon grows.

This is important for the thesis because the whole point of the model is not only to forecast accurately, but to forecast in a calibrated and uncertainty-aware way.

## 5. How K2VAE Is Structured

The architecture in the paper can be understood as a pipeline with a clear conceptual purpose.

1. The input sequence is patched into tokens.
2. A learned measurement function $\psi$ maps those tokens into a representation where the dynamics are easier to linearise.
3. A Koopman-inspired transition operator is fitted to describe the temporal evolution in that space.
4. A KalmanNet-style module refines the resulting linear system using residual nonlinear information and outputs both a refined state and a covariance matrix.
5. A decoder maps the latent samples back into the original space and parameterises the predictive distribution.

The important conceptual point is that K2VAE does not simply learn a generic sequence-to-sequence map. It tries to learn a latent dynamical system that is both structured and probabilistic.

The basic training objective can be written as:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{ELBO} + \lambda \|X - X_{rec}\|_2^2
$$

where the first term handles probabilistic forecasting and the second term encourages the measurement space to become more linear and consistent with the reconstruction target.

The project should be approached in stages so that it remains interpretable.

I should first ensure that the data pipeline, forecasting setup, and evaluation metrics are all working properly. This means checking:

The model should be compared against a small but meaningful set of baselines, including:

Ablations are useful, but they should be targeted. The key questions are:

- does the Koopman-inspired component help?

These are the experiments that sharpen the thesis story.

The final step is not only to report numbers. I need to explain what the results mean in terms of the underlying model assumptions and the forecasting problem itself.

- structured temporal modelling rather than purely opaque forecasting;
- explicit uncertainty handling;
- potential advantage on long horizons;
- a clear conceptual bridge between classical dynamical systems and modern deep learning.

- the Koopman approximation may be imperfect;
- training may be unstable;

- and the latent representation may not always be easy to interpret.

These risks are not reasons to avoid the project. They are part of what makes the project interesting.

## 10. Open Questions and Working Hypotheses

These are the main research questions I want to keep in view while working on the project. Each one is important not only because it is interesting, but because it directly affects how convincing the thesis story will be.

1. Does K2VAE actually improve long-horizon forecasting in practice?

   This is the most important question. The motivation for the model is that long-horizon forecasting suffers from error accumulation and unstable dynamics, and K2VAE is meant to address this by imposing a more structured transition process. My expectation is that K2VAE should perform better than a weaker baseline on longer horizons, especially when the dynamics are not trivial. I would be interested to see whether the gain is strong enough to justify the extra architectural complexity.

2. Is the Koopman-inspired component genuinely helpful, or is it mainly a conceptual idea with limited practical value?

   The Koopman part is central to the model, but it is also the part that could fail if the learned representation does not actually expose a simpler dynamical structure. My hypothesis is that the Koopman-inspired component should help when the data contain underlying structure that is easier to model in a transformed space. If the data are too irregular or too noisy, then the benefit may be small.

3. Does the Kalman-style refinement improve stability and uncertainty calibration?

   The Kalman refinement is meant to correct the imperfect linearisation and to produce a more principled uncertainty estimate. My expectation is that it should improve robustness, especially over longer horizons where small modelling errors can accumulate. I also expect it to help the model produce more calibrated uncertainty, rather than just sharper but misleading forecasts.

4. How sensitive is the model to data smoothness, noise, and sequence length?

   This question matters because the method depends on assumptions about temporal structure. I expect K2VAE to work best on datasets where the dynamics are reasonably smooth and structured, and less well on highly irregular, chaotic, or very noisy sequences. I also expect performance to degrade more slowly than simpler models when the horizon grows, but this is something that needs to be tested rather than assumed.

5. Is the probabilistic formulation genuinely useful, or is it mostly extra complexity?

   The probabilistic component is one of the main reasons K2VAE is interesting, but it also adds difficulty. My hypothesis is that the probabilistic formulation should provide real value in terms of uncertainty estimates and calibration, not just in terms of predictive likelihood. If the model produces better uncertainty behaviour without sacrificing point forecast quality, then the probabilistic modelling is justified.

6. Can the learned latent dynamics be meaningfully interpreted?

   This is a more secondary question, but it still matters for the thesis. I expect that the latent representation will not be as interpretable as a classical dynamical system, but it may still reveal structure that is useful for understanding the temporal evolution. The question is whether the learned state space is informative enough to support a meaningful interpretation, or whether it remains mostly a practical device.

7. Is K2VAE strong enough to support a convincing thesis narrative?

   This is the broader evaluative question. My expectation is that the answer is yes if the model shows a clear and consistent advantage on the right type of tasks. The strongest thesis story would be that structured latent dynamics can improve probabilistic forecasting in a principled way, rather than simply by adding more complexity.

K2VAE is a strong project direction because it combines three ideas that are all relevant to the forecasting problem: structured dynamics, learned representation, and probabilistic modelling. The main appeal is that it does not treat forecasting as a purely black-box task. It tries to make the temporal evolution more understandable and more stable by using a Koopman-inspired linearisation and a Kalman-style refinement step.

For my own project, the most important thing is to keep the focus on the real contribution: not just building a model that predicts well, but understanding whether a structured latent dynamics model can improve probabilistic forecasting in a principled and meaningful way.
