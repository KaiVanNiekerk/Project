NOTE_ON_VALIDATION

Summary:
The codebase is currently optimizing a likelihood-based objective, not a CRPS-based objective. In the K2VAE path, training loss is built from the ELBO / negative log-likelihood (NLL) term, plus KL and reconstruction terms; in the baseline PatchTST path, evaluation computes CRPS separately as a test-time metric, but the training objective is still NLL-based. This means the raw loss curves are expected to be dominated by NLL, especially when the sigma (uncertainty) term is poorly calibrated.

Why NLL dominates training curves:
- NLL is extremely sensitive to uncertainty calibration, not just point accuracy.
- If sigma is underestimated, the log-likelihood becomes very large (negative log probability) even when the mean forecast is reasonable.
- The NLL term therefore often dwarfs the scale of rec or KL in the plotted loss curves.
- This is a property of the objective, not necessarily evidence that the model is failing at forecasting.

CRPS implementation:
The CRPS function in src/metrics.py is the standard empirical CRPS identity:
    CRPS(F_n, x) = E|X - x| - 0.5 * E|X - X'|
This is mathematically valid for empirical predictive samples and is used as a proper scoring rule for probabilistic forecasting evaluation.

Important caveat:
- CRPS is computed from sampled predictive draws and represents a distributional quality metric.
- It is not the same as the training objective being optimized.
- So a model can have a large NLL loss while still producing competitive CRPS / NMAE values.

Why tuning does not remove the effect completely:
- Hyperparameter tuning changes the optimization trade-offs, but it does not change the fact that the training objective is likelihood-based.
- If the model remains miscalibrated or overconfident, the NLL term will still dominate.
- This means the loss curve should be interpreted as an optimization signal, not as a direct measure of downstream forecast quality.

Practical interpretation for comparison tables:
Use NMAE and CRPS as the primary comparison metrics for forecasting quality, because they are the metrics aligned with the paper's evaluation goals.
The loss curve can still be useful for diagnosing training stability and calibration, but it should not be read as a one-to-one indicator of final forecasting performance.

Conclusion:
The current setup is internally consistent: the project is optimizing NLL while evaluating CRPS and NMAE as reporting metrics. The NLL-heavy curves are therefore expected behavior under the objective, rather than an obvious calculation error.

[AMENDMENT] The "internally consistent" claim above holds WITHIN the K2VAE path but does NOT hold ACROSS models. See "Checkpoint selection is asymmetric" below.


================================================================
CHECKPOINT SELECTION IS ASYMMETRIC ACROSS MODELS
================================================================

K2VAE and the baselines do not select their best checkpoint on the same
criterion:

- K2VAE (src/train.py, _checkpoint_step called with val_metrics["total"]):
  selects on the composite loss, NLL + beta*KL + lambda*Rec.
- PatchTST / Koopa (baselines/baseline_trainer.py): _train_epoch and
  _validate return {"total": avg_nll, "elbo": 0.0, "kl": 0.0, "rec": 0.0},
  so "total" IS pure NLL. These select on forecast likelihood alone.

We therefore pick the baselines' best forecasting checkpoint but K2VAE's
best ELBO checkpoint, then compare all three on CRPS. The KL and
reconstruction terms pull the K2VAE checkpoint away from the reported
metric. This is not a shared handicap; it penalises only the model under
test, which is exactly the kind of asymmetry that can flip a ranking.

Supporting evidence: ETTh1-L at L=720 has best_epoch=2 (val composite loss
never improved after epoch 2, early stop at epoch 7) and is also our worst
result, CRPS 0.5384.

Cheapest test: checkpoint K2VAE on val_metrics["nll"] instead of
val_metrics["total"] and rerun ETTh1 L=720. One-line change, and it makes
the comparison against our own baselines like-for-like for the first time.


================================================================
DATA SPLIT PROTOCOL: DIVERGENCE FROM ProbTS / THE K2VAE PAPER
================================================================

Summary:
Our loader applies a single 70/15/15 chronological split to every dataset
(CLI.load, train_frac=0.7, val_frac=0.15). The paper evaluates under ProbTS,
which does NOT use one ratio. ProbTS branches by dataset type and none of
its branches yield 70/15/15. Our numbers are therefore measured on different
data segments than the published table, so direct comparison against the
paper's figures is currently invalid.

What ProbTS does (probts/data/data_utils/get_datasets.py, function
get_dataset_borders):

- ETTh1 / ETTh2: hardcoded month borders, the ratio arguments are ignored.
      border_begin = [0, 12*30*24, 12*30*24 + 4*30*24]
      border_end   = [12*30*24, 12*30*24 + 4*30*24, 12*30*24 + 8*30*24]
  That is 12 months train / 4 validation / 4 test.

- Other long-term datasets (Weather, Electricity, Traffic, Exchange-L):
  ratio based, defaults train_ratio=0.7 and test_ratio=0.2, validation takes
  the remainder. That is 70/10/20, not 70/15/15.

- Short-term datasets (Exchange-S, Solar-S, ...): not ratio based at all.
  See the Exchange-S section below.

(ETTm borders were not investigated, ETTm is out of scope for this project.)

Concrete consequence for ETTh1, 17,420 rows:

    ProbTS   train[0,8640)   val[8640,11520)   test[11520,14400)
    Ours     train[0,12194)  val[12194,14807)  test[14807,17420)

- ProbTS discards the final 3,020 rows, 17.3% of the file, entirely.
- Our test set begins 407 rows AFTER the ProbTS test set ends.
- The two test sets have ZERO overlap.
- Rows 11520-12194, which ProbTS holds out as test data, sit inside our
  training set.

We are scoring a different, later segment of a non-stationary series. This
is the most likely explanation for ALL THREE of our models (K2VAE, PatchTST,
Koopa) scoring worse than their published counterparts, since the offset
applies symmetrically to models we did not write:

    avg CRPS      ours     paper
    ETTh1 K2VAE   0.4385   0.314
    ETTh1 PatchTST 0.3923  0.323
    ETTh1 Koopa   0.4025   0.318

Important: this does NOT explain why K2VAE underperforms OUR OWN PatchTST
and Koopa runs. Those share our loader exactly (baselines/run_baseline.py
calls the same CLI.load), so the split hits all three identically. The
internal ranking flip needs an asymmetric cause, which is the checkpoint
selection criterion above and/or the Koopman spectral-norm clamp
(max_operator_norm=1.0, applied ~34 times at L=720).

Consistency check: on Wind-L, our own dataset with no published reference
split, K2VAE wins on average (0.6057 vs PatchTST 0.6092, Koopa 0.6238).


================================================================
EXCHANGE-S: WRONG SOURCE FILE AND WRONG PROTOCOL
================================================================

Two independent problems.

1. We are loading the wrong file.

   Paper dataset table:  Exchange-S = 6,071 timesteps
                         Exchange-L = 7,588 timesteps
   Our datasets/exchange_rate/exchange_rate.csv = 7,588 rows (verified,
   spanning 1990/1/1 to 2010/10/10), an exact match for Exchange-L.

   Our Exchange-S config applies the Exchange-S protocol (T = L = 30) to the
   Exchange-L data. We are comparing against the wrong column of the paper.

2. Exchange-S in ProbTS never touches the ratio-based path.

   Short-term datasets route to prepare_STSF_dataset, which calls GluonTS
   datasets.get_dataset("exchange_rate_nips") and uses the split baked into
   that archive.

   GluonTS exchange_rate_nips (_gp_copula_2019.py):
       prediction_length = 30, freq = "B", num_series = 8,
       rolling_evaluations = 5

   The test portion is 5 rolling windows of 30 steps appended to the end of
   the train series. ProbTS then carves validation out of the train series
   (split_train_val in data_utils.py):
       offset = num_test_windows * prediction_length
       trunc_train_len = train_len - offset
   which removes a further 5 * 30 = 150 steps.

   Taking the paper's 6,071 total, the implied structure is approximately:

       train   steps 0-5771      (~95%)
       val     steps 5771-5921   (150 steps, 5 windows of 30)
       test    steps 5921-6071   (150 steps, 5 windows of 30)

   The absolute train length is inferred from the paper's 6,071 figure; the
   structure itself comes directly from the ProbTS and GluonTS source.

   This is structurally unlike any ratio split. Evaluation is over 5 fixed
   rolling windows of 30 steps, not the ~1,100 stride-1 sliding windows our
   loader produces.

Conclusion: our Exchange-S results are not comparable to the paper's
Exchange-S column on two independent grounds, wrong source file and a
different evaluation protocol.
